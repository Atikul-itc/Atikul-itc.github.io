Remote sensing · Dryland ecosystems

Atikul Hoque

PhD Candidate, Geography and Environmental Studies, New Mexico State University

I study how woody plants are reshaping desert grasslands and how we can measure that change from space. My dissertation pairs centimeter-scale drone imagery with Sentinel-1 radar and Sentinel-2 time series, using deep learning to map shrub cover across the Chihuahuan Desert.

Alongside the dissertation, I build open drought monitoring tools, including EcoDRI, a weekly ecological drought index for the conterminous United States. I work with Dr. Michaela Buenemann at the Spatial Applications Research Center. Before coming to NMSU, I completed an MSc at ITC, University of Twente, and interned at the German Aerospace Center (DLR).

Research focus

All projects →
01

Woody plant encroachment

Mapping mesquite expansion into desert grasslands, from individual shrubs in drone imagery to fractional cover at landscape scale.

02

Radar and optical fusion

Attention-based networks that combine Sentinel-1 backscatter with Sentinel-2 reflectance to retrieve shrub cover where either sensor alone falls short.

03

Drought monitoring

Operational indices that weigh vegetation, temperature, and soil moisture signals differently across climate zones.

04

Vegetation phenology

Time series methods for retrieving crop and rangeland growth stages from optical and radar observations.
