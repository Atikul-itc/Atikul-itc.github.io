Research

Measuring change in drylands

Woody plant encroachment has converted large areas of semi-arid grassland into shrubland, with consequences for forage, carbon, erosion, and fire. My work develops methods that can track this conversion consistently across scales, and connects it to drought and climate variability.

Dissertation

Mapping and Monitoring of Shrub-Encroached Vegetation in Drylands Using Remote Sensing
Chapter 1

What two decades of studies tell us about mapping shrubs

A systematic review of 174 studies and 234 sensor and method configurations on remote sensing of woody vegetation in global drylands. The review examines how sensor choice and spatial resolution have shaped the mapping of shrub encroachment, and which questions remain open.

Chapter 2

Separating green, dry, and bare surfaces from drone imagery

A hybrid CNN-MLP that classifies photosynthetic vegetation, non-photosynthetic vegetation, and bare soil in UAS imagery of mesquite-encroached rangeland at the Jornada Experimental Range. Models are evaluated with leave-one-site-out cross-validation across 13 sites, and the analysis tests how spatial resolution and feature sets change accuracy.

Chapter 3

Fractional shrub cover from Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2

An attention-based CNN fuses Sentinel-1 backscatter with Sentinel-2 reflectance to estimate fractional shrub cover on the 10 m grid. Reference cover comes from the Chapter 2 classifications aggregated to Sentinel-2 pixels. Fusion models are compared against single-sensor CNNs and a Random Forest baseline.

Chapter 4

When should we look? Climate-informed retrieval timing

Shrubs and grasses green up at different times, so the acquisition date changes how separable they are. This ongoing chapter uses Sentinel-2 time series and climate records to identify when fractional shrub cover can be retrieved most reliably, and how that window varies between years.

Other projects

EcoDRI: Ecological Drought Index

A weekly, 5 km drought index for the conterminous United States (2001–2025). It blends vegetation, thermal, and soil moisture condition with 90-day precipitation anomalies, weighted by Köppen–Geiger climate zone, and is calibrated against the U.S. Drought Monitor.
